Why it matters

One file per broadcast

With every broadcast in the archive once, a question is answered from one copy of the evidence instead of two, and a citation cannot point to a re-upload with the wrong date. It also makes video links easier to get right: each stream now pairs with one playlist entry, and 573 of the 605 links found were confirmed by checking the transcript length against the video's.

How the duplicates were found

Compared by what was said

Compare what was said, not what the file is called

Many streams had been re-downloaded from other channels, so their file names carried the wrong date and time. The scanner ignores names and turns each transcript into a set of five-word phrases, then looks for pairs of streams that share a large part of them.

Catch cut-down re-uploads

The score is the share of the smaller stream that appears in the larger one, not the overlap of both. A trimmed or partial re-upload of a longer stream still scores high, where an ordinary overlap score would miss it.

Ignore stock phrases

Phrases that turn up in a large share of all streams (“thank you very much for the”) are left out, so two unrelated streams are not linked just because the host says the same things every week.

Find the original

Each stream is matched against the YouTube playlist the same way the index does it. When one side of a pair has a real video link, it is flagged as the original Francis upload, and those pairs come first in the review.

A person decides

The scan only reads files. It writes a review page with the two titles side by side, how much they overlap, their lengths and an Open in YouTube button for each, and nothing is deleted until someone has looked.

Rebuilt from scratch

A fresh index

After the deletions the search index was thrown away and built again from the transcripts on disk, so nothing from a removed stream is left in it. The summaries already written were carried across for every stream whose transcript had not changed, and the few streams without one were summarised again. Streams too short to summarise, such as a clip with no speech, still have none.

Good to know

Limits worth knowing about

No new features

Search, Ask, summaries and characters work exactly as they did in the last release. What changed is the archive underneath them.

One pair is still open

A version of a 2024 stream with the dead air trimmed overlaps its full-length original by about 85%. It is kept until someone decides which to keep.

Not every stream has a video link

141 streams still have no YouTube link on file, and 32 of the 605 links found could not have their timestamps checked against the video length.

A few transcripts are being redone

A handful are being re-transcribed, so they may still read like the older version until they are replaced.
