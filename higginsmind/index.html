Transcription accuracy

Fewer invented lines, everywhere

Whisper's failure mode on long streams is to loop: the same short phrase written down hundreds of times over silence, music or game audio. The measures used are the share of lines that repeat an earlier line exactly, and the longest unbroken run of identical lines.

What the old model was making up

2026-09-15 (2) - More misery

One flight-simulator radio phrase and the number after it were written 1,151 times each, in strict alternation, between minutes 120 and 160. Parakeet has ordinary speech there instead.

2025-04-15 (2) - Verdansk '25

The old transcript ran to 284 minutes. The video is 3 hours 45 minutes, so the last 59 minutes of that transcript described nothing that exists.

2021-08-13 - Shlug ~ Life

"Thank you." written 149 times over roughly an hour of silence.

2023-06-04 (1) - Wash and Wax

Ended with "Transcription by CastingWords" repeated 30 times, a well-known Whisper artifact.

Word count fell 11% overall (15.1M to 13.5M). That is mostly the invented filler being removed, so a lower count is the intended result. The measures count lines, not correctness, and a real recurring bit (a soundboard clip, a repeated joke) can legitimately repeat, which is why the after figure is not zero.

Everything else

Shipped alongside

Copy an answer to share

A Copy answer button on the Ask page copies the answer as italic markdown, with every citation linked to the exact moment on YouTube. The links are written so Discord does not unfold each one into a video player and fill the channel.

A summary for every stream

Open a stream and its summary is there, or you can ask for one to be written. Nearly every stream now has one, and new streams get theirs automatically as they are added.

A rebuilt index

The whole archive was re-chunked and re-embedded from the new transcripts, so search and Ask work from cleaner text: 785 streams and 59,280 passages. Before, there were 780 streams and 53,471 passages, a fifth of them padding.

Characters, relearned

The four character profiles were rewritten from the cleaner transcripts, so they describe how each one actually talks instead of picking up the old model's mistakes.

Made for phones

Library rows stack on a small screen, the heatmap swipes and its days are big enough to tap, and typing in a filter no longer makes iOS zoom in. The nav fits five buttons on a phone.

A sharper library

The heatmap can be saved as an image that follows the active host, tag and date filters, drawn in the same style as the app. Hide empty condenses it to only the months that had a stream.

See where a stream came from

Each stream page shows which speech-to-text model made its transcript and which AI wrote its summary, so it is clear what you are reading.

Better tags

Episodes of Computer Chronicles and Net Café that were missing their tag can now be found from the words around them, so the archive's tags are more complete.

Testing before changing the AI

A newer AI model is tried on the same streams and the same questions as the current one before anything changes. The latest test found it more careful about not inventing an answer and better at covering a long stream, but about twice as slow.

Good to know

Limits worth knowing about

Answers can be wrong

Answers come from an AI reading transcripts, and the transcripts are machine-made too. Open the linked moment to check a quote before relying on it.

Links start a little early

A citation link opens the video at the start of the passage it cites, about 5 seconds early for context. Passages run a couple of minutes, so the line being quoted can come a little way in.

A few streams are being redone

A handful of transcripts are being re-transcribed, so they may still read like the older version until they are replaced.

Not every stream has a video link

Some streams have no YouTube link on file. Their citations show the stream and time, but cannot jump to a video.

A few streams have no summary

Three are too short to summarise: a 15-second clip, one with no speech, and one under 200 words.
